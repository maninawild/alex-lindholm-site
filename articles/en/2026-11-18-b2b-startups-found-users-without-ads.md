B2B startups like Supabase, Cal.com, and Daytona didn’t rely on paid ads to scale up — they secured thousands of early users and went on to raise multi-million dollar seed rounds by going straight to where their developers and buyers already lived online.

Here is the exact playbook they used:

1. **Supabase**
   - Channel: Hacker News
   - Traction: 1,400+ signups in just 7 days
   - Outcome: Raised a $6M seed round 5 months later
2. **Cal.com**
   - Channels: Product Hunt and GitHub
   - Traction: Named #1 Product of the Month, scaling to 10,000 weekly users
   - Outcome: Secured a $7.4M seed round
3. **Daytona**
   - Channel: Open-source launch via GitHub
   - Traction: Hit 2,000+ stars in 48 hours, nearly doubling ARR in 6 months
   - Outcome: Closed a $5M seed round

The underlying lesson is simple: none of these teams bought user acquisition. They planted their flag where their target audience spent time, converted that initial wave into active users, and used real metrics to prove market demand.

That is precisely what investors look for at the seed stage. They aren’t buying a polished slide deck — they want proof that people actually want what you are building.

Before you start pitching, ask yourself two straightforward questions:

- Where do your buyers actually hang out online?
- What would make them try your product this week?
