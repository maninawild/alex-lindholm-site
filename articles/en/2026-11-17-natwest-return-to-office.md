Starting next February, 13,000 NatWest employees are facing a mandatory shift from just two office days a month to two days every single week. Unsurprisingly, the union is pushing back.

I get it. Nobody enjoys being forced back onto a crowded commute.

At the same time, NatWest is far from an outlier. Amazon moved corporate staff back five days a week back in early 2025, and JPMorgan brought hybrid workers back full-time shortly after. Compared to that wave, two days a week is honestly getting off lightly.

Like it or not, large employers are steadily pulling teams back in — and I don’t think it is just traditional management missing the sight of occupied desks.

- **Velocity drops:** Complex ideas move noticeably slower when every single conversation requires a scheduled calendar invite.
- **Friction builds:** A problem that could be resolved in five minutes across a desk stretches into three days of asynchronous Slack messages, losing vital context along the way.
- **Junior talent suffers most:** Early-career professionals learn just as much by overhearing senior colleagues handle difficult situations or getting pulled into spontaneous whiteboard sessions as they do from formal training.
- **Culture erodes:** It is difficult to build genuine institutional loyalty toward a team you only know as tiny video squares on a screen.

Running operations and working alongside teams across Europe, I would back people collaborating physically in the room every single time.

NatWest staff might be frustrated right now, but I reckon a year from now, they’ll wonder what all the fuss was about.
