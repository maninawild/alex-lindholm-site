Everyone predicted AI would hollow out the job market.

So far, it is doing the exact opposite - sorry for layed-off crowds: the Economist estimates AI has created roughly one million jobs in America alone, while AI-attributed layoffs since mid-2023 sit around two hundred thousand.

The new roles go far beyond pure AI engineering:

- Data center construction now runs above $75B annually, driving massive demand for grid engineers, electricians, and technicians.
- LinkedIn estimates over 600,000 AI-specific roles opened up between 2023 and 2025.
- Even roles marked as vulnerable are growing, with paralegal employment up 11 percent and market researchers up 6 percent over the same period.

Naturally, there are casualties. Customer service and administrative roles are down ten to fifteen percent.

Technologies rarely just erase work. They shift bottlenecks.

More compute demands energy infrastructure. More automation demands human supervision, deployment, and integration.

The future of work is not a zero-sum replacement game. It is a messy transition where certain tasks disappear while entirely new layers of the economy get built.
