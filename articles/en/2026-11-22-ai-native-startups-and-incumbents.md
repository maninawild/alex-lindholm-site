Marc Andreessen recently pointed out something striking: the gap between what AI-native startups and traditional incumbents believe artificial intelligence can achieve is the widest he has ever witnessed in tech.

His exact observation captures the shift:

> “The AI-native early stage startups have just a completely different view on what they can do with AI than the typical incumbents in almost any industry.”

It is not a minor divergence. It is a completely different worldview.

This highlights the real danger facing established enterprises today. The primary threat is not slow technological adoption — it is a fundamental miscalibration of what AI is actually capable of. The early-stage companies entering their markets have zero legacy assumptions holding them back.

This asymmetry compounds rapidly:

- Incumbents build roadmaps constrained by today’s operational floor.
- Startups build entire business models around tomorrow’s technological ceiling.

By the time legacy roadmaps catch up to what is already possible at the ceiling, the market has shifted entirely.
