Nobody has ever enjoyed going to a bank.

Back in 2013, the pitch behind N26 felt deceptively simple: people live on their phones, so put the bank there. No branches, no paperwork, no waiting for an appointment.

Obvious in hindsight. Not obvious then.

Building an interface is easy. Securing a banking licence to hold other people's money takes years of regulatory battles - and most challengers die in that exact gap.

N26 made it through, scaling to twenty-five markets and millions of customers.

Yet the real asset is not the valuation or the app interface. It is the fifteen hundred people who built it.

They are the ones who learned which regulators ruin a quarter, which growth curves are illusions, and what breaks at three million users that ran smoothly at three hundred thousand.

Ten years ago, finding operators in Berlin who had scaled something truly complex was a short exercise. Today, that diaspora is the real engine of the ecosystem - operators who have been in the trenches when things broke, now building the next wave.
